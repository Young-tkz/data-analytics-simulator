TOPIC 1 — DESCRIPTIVE STATISTICS & DATA VISUALIZATION
==================================================

EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS (EDA)

EDA means exploring, examining and visualizing a dataset to understand its structure, patterns and relationships between variables.

It helps answer questions such as:
- Which students scored highest?
- Which products sell the most?
- What patterns exist in the data?
- Are there unusual observations or outliers?

Example:
A college can use EDA to find whether higher attendance is linked to higher marks.


OBJECTIVES OF EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS

1. Understand the Dataset
   - Know the dataset structure.
   - Understand what each variable represents.

2. Identify Missing Values
   - Find gaps in the data before analysis.

3. Detect Duplicates
   - Find records that appear more than once.

4. Discover Relationships
   - See how variables move or change together.

5. Understand Trends
   - Notice patterns and changes across the data.

6. Find Outliers
   - Locate unusual values that do not fit the general pattern.

7. Prepare for Analysis
   - Leave the dataset ready for deeper statistical analysis.


STEPS IN EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS

1. Collect Data
2. Understand Data
3. Clean Data
4. Calculate Statistics
5. Create Charts
6. Identify Patterns
7. Make Decisions


TYPES OF EXPLORATORY DATA ANALYSIS

UNIVARIATE ANALYSIS
- Studies only one variable.
- Example: Student marks.
- Can calculate average, highest and lowest marks.

BIVARIATE ANALYSIS
- Studies two variables together.
- Example: Attendance and marks.
- Helps determine whether the two variables move together.

MULTIVARIATE ANALYSIS
- Studies three or more variables together.
- Example: Attendance, marks, study hours and assignment scores.


CORE COMPONENTS OF EDA

UNDERSTANDING THE DATASET
- Number of rows and columns
- Data types
- Variable names

Example:
Rows = students
Columns = information about students

DETECTING DUPLICATE DATA
- The same record appears more than once.
- Usually one copy should be removed.

CHECKING MISSING VALUES
- Data may be incomplete.
- A NULL entry means data is missing.

DETECTING OUTLIERS
- Outliers are unusual values.
- Example: Marks = 75, 80, 82, 85, 1000
- 1000 is an obvious outlier.


DESCRIPTIVE STATISTICS

Descriptive statistics summarize and describe existing data.


MEASURES OF CENTRAL TENDENCY

MEAN
- The average.
- Formula:
  Mean = Sum of all values / Number of values

Example:
40, 50, 50, 60, 100
Mean = 60

Use:
- Summarizing overall class performance.

MEDIAN
- The middle value after arranging data in order.

Example:
40, 50, 50, 70, 80
Median = 50

Use:
- Useful when extreme values may distort the average.

MODE
- The value that occurs most frequently.

Example:
10, 20, 20, 20, 30
Mode = 20

Use:
- Useful for finding the most common category or value.


MEASURES OF DISPERSION

RANGE
- Highest value - Lowest value.

Example:
40, 50, 50, 70, 80
Range = 80 - 40 = 40

VARIANCE
- Measures how far data values are from the average.
- Small variance = values are close together.
- Large variance = values are spread out.

STANDARD DEVIATION
- Measures how much values differ from the mean.
- Small standard deviation = values are close to the mean.
- Large standard deviation = values are more spread out.


DATA VISUALIZATION

Data visualization means representing data using graphs, charts, maps and diagrams.

Benefits:
- Helps identify patterns quickly.
- Makes outliers stand out visually.
- Makes reports easier to understand.
- Supports faster decision-making.


COMMON CHART TYPES

BAR CHART
- Compares values between categories.
- Example: Sales of different products or department performance.

LINE CHART
- Shows trends over time.
- Example: Monthly sales, daily temperature or website visitors.

PIE CHART
- Shows how a whole is divided into parts.
- Example: Market share across four companies.

HISTOGRAM
- Shows the frequency distribution of numerical data grouped into ranges.
- Example: Marks grouped into ranges.

SCATTER PLOT
- Shows the relationship between two numerical variables.
- Example: Study hours vs marks.

BOX PLOT
- Summarizes minimum, maximum, median, quartiles and outliers.


CHART SELECTION

Compare categories:
- Bar Chart

Show a trend over time:
- Line Chart

Show percentage/share:
- Pie Chart

Show distribution:
- Histogram

Show relationship:
- Scatter Plot


CASE STUDY — STUDENT PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS

A college collected data from 500 students, including:
- Attendance
- Internal marks
- Final examination marks
- Assignment scores

Analysis:
- Removed missing values.
- Detected duplicate records.
- Found students with unusually low attendance.
- Studied the relationship between attendance and marks.

Result and impact:
- Attendance was linked with higher scores.
- Most failures had attendance below 60%.
- Attendance monitoring and extra coaching were introduced.
- Student performance improved the next semester.


ADVANTAGES OF EDA AND DATA VISUALIZATION

- Helps understand data quickly and thoroughly.
- Detects errors before deeper analysis.
- Improves data quality and reliability.
- Makes reports easier to understand.
- Supports better and faster business decisions.
- Identifies trends and hidden patterns early.


TOPIC 1 KEY TAKEAWAYS

- EDA helps understand a dataset before deeper analysis.
- EDA involves collecting, understanding, cleaning, calculating statistics, creating charts and identifying patterns.
- Central tendency includes mean, median and mode.
- Dispersion includes range, variance and standard deviation.
- Visualization makes patterns and outliers easier to identify.

TOPIC 2 — DATA CLEANING & OUTLIER DETECTION
==================================================

WHAT IS DATA CLEANING?

Data cleaning is the process of finding and correcting problems in data before analysis.

Good analysis depends on good-quality data.

Incorrect data entered
    ->
Incorrect output produced


OBJECTIVES OF DATA CLEANING

- Improve overall data quality.
- Remove incorrect information.
- Increase the accuracy of analysis.
- Save time and reduce recurring errors.
- Improve decision-making.
- Prepare data for machine learning.


COMMON DATA QUALITY PROBLEMS

MISSING DATA
- Information is not available.
- Example: A hospital patient forgets to mention their blood group.

INCORRECT DATA
- Values are entered incorrectly.
- Example: 5,000 entered as 50,000.

DUPLICATE DATA
- The same record appears twice.
- Example: One customer creates two accounts with the same information.

INCONSISTENT DATA
- The same information appears in different formats.
- Example:
  Ahmedabad
  Amdavad
  AHMEDABAD

INVALID DATA
- A value breaks business rules.
- Example: Attendance recorded as more than 100%.


STEPS IN DATA CLEANING

1. Identify Errors
2. Handle Missing Values
3. Remove Duplicate Records
4. Correct and Standardize Values
5. Validate Data
6. Produce Clean Data Ready for Analysis


HANDLING MISSING VALUES

1. REMOVE THE RECORD
- If only a few records have missing values, delete them entirely.

2. REPLACE WITH MEAN
- Replace the missing value with the average of available values.

Example:
70, 80, NULL, 70
Mean of available values = 73.33
NULL can be replaced with 73.33.

3. REPLACE WITH MEDIAN
- Replace the missing value with the median of the available values.

4. REPLACE WITH MODE
- Replace the missing value with the most frequently occurring value.

Example:
Blood Group = A, B, A, NULL
Replace NULL with A.


REMOVING DUPLICATES

- Duplicate records increase storage requirements.
- They can distort analysis.

Example:
Customer ID 01 — Amit
Customer ID 01 — Amit

Keep one record.


CORRECTING INCORRECT DATA

Fix values that were entered incorrectly.

Example:
Salary shown as 5,00,00,000 instead of 50,000.

Applications:
- Banks remove duplicate customer records before preparing statements.
- Airlines correct passport numbers before issuing boarding passes.


STANDARDIZING DATA

Standardizing means putting data into one consistent format.

Example:
Ahmedabad
Amdavad
AHMEDABAD

Can be standardized to:
Ahmedabad

Organizations standardize department names, locations and other fields consistently.


VALIDATING DATA

Validation checks whether data follows predefined business rules.

Examples:
- Age must be between 12 and 60.
- Salary must be greater than 0.
- Attendance must be between 0% and 100%.
- A phone number field should contain a valid phone number format.


WHAT IS AN OUTLIER?

An outlier is a data value that is very different from the rest of the observations.

Example:
80, 82, 85, 90, 1000

1000 is an outlier.


WHY DO OUTLIERS OCCUR?

1. Data entry mistakes
   - Typo or extra zero creates an impossible value.

2. Measurement errors
   - Faulty instruments record incorrect readings.

3. Human error
   - Manual entry mistakes during data collection.

4. Equipment failure
   - Example: A temperature sensor records 150°C when it should not.

5. Genuine rare events
   - Some outliers represent real but unusual events.


WHY OUTLIER DETECTION MATTERS

Example:
Marks = 80, 82, 85, 90, 1000

An extreme value such as 1000 can greatly affect the average and produce misleading results.


METHODS OF OUTLIER DETECTION

1. VISUAL METHOD
- Charts make outliers easy to spot.
- Examples:
  - Box plot
  - Scatter plot
  - Histogram
- Banks can use dashboards to flag unusually large transactions.

2. Z-SCORE METHOD
- Measures how far a value is from the mean in terms of standard deviations.

3. STATISTICAL METHOD — IQR
- Interquartile Range compares values with the spread of the middle 50% of the data.

4. DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE
- Experts identify unusual values using knowledge of the field.
- Example: 150 marks when the maximum is 100.


HANDLING OUTLIERS

REMOVE THE OUTLIER
- If the value is caused by an error.

CORRECT THE VALUE
- If the correct value is known.

Example:
Salary entered as 5,00,000 instead of 50,000.

KEEP THE OUTLIER
- If it represents a genuine event.

Example:
A customer makes an unusually large purchase.
Do not automatically remove it.


NOISE VS OUTLIERS

NOISE
- Random error in data.
- Usually small errors.
- Usually removed.

OUTLIER
- An unusual observation.
- Can be an extreme value.
- May be removed or retained depending on the situation.


CASE STUDY — HOSPITAL PATIENT DATA

A hospital collects patient information and finds impossible values during a routine data audit.

Problems:
- Age recorded as 200 years.
- Temperature recorded as 250°F.

Actions:
- Age corrected using patient records.
- Temperature rechecked using medical equipment.
- Data validated before storage.

Result:
- Cleaned data becomes reliable for medical analysis.


APPLICATIONS OF DATA CLEANING & OUTLIER DETECTION

Banking:
- Fraud detection
- Duplicate customer removal

Healthcare:
- Cleaning patient records
- Identifying abnormal readings

Education:
- Correcting student attendance and marks

Retail:
- Removing duplicate customers
- Correcting sales data

Manufacturing:
- Detecting faulty machine sensor readings

Transportation:
- Correcting GPS data
- Identifying unusual travel patterns


DATA CLEANING VS OUTLIER DETECTION

DATA CLEANING
- Improves overall data quality.
- Removes errors and duplicates.
- Handles missing and inconsistent data.
- Makes data ready for analysis.

OUTLIER DETECTION
- Finds unusual values.
- Identifies abnormal observations.
- Focuses on extreme values.
- Prevents misleading analysis.


TOPIC 2 KEY TAKEAWAYS

- Data cleaning improves the quality and reliability of data.
- Cleaning follows a clear pipeline from raw data to analysis-ready data.
- Outliers may be errors or genuine rare events.
- Common problems include missing, duplicate, incorrect, inconsistent and invalid data.
- Clean and accurate data leads to better decisions.

TOPIC 3 — EXPLOITING PATTERNS & TRENDS IN DATA
==================================================

WHY PATTERNS & TRENDS MATTER

- Understand customer behaviour and anticipate what they may do next.
- Predict future events and improve forecasting accuracy.
- Improve decisions, profits and risk management.

Examples:
- A supermarket notices bread and butter are often bought together.
- A bank detects unusual transaction patterns that may indicate fraud.


PATTERN VS TREND

PATTERN
- A repeated or predictable relationship in data.
- Something that happens repeatedly.
- Shows relationships between items or events.

Example:
Customers who buy bread often buy butter.

TREND
- The general direction data moves over time.
- Shows whether something rises, falls or stays stable.
- Reveals growth or decline across a period.

Example:
Monthly online sales continuously increasing.


TYPES OF PATTERNS

ASSOCIATION PATTERNS
- Relationships between items.
- Example: Mobile phones and phone covers are often bought together.
- Recommendation systems use this information.

CLASSIFICATION PATTERNS
- Grouping data into categories.
- Examples:
  - Loan applications: Approved / Rejected
  - Email: Spam / Not Spam

SEQUENTIAL PATTERNS
- Events occur in a particular order.
- Example:
  Search -> Add to Cart -> Purchase
- Used for recommendations.

CLUSTERING PATTERNS
- Groups similar records together.
- Examples:
  - Students
  - Professionals
  - Customers


TYPES OF TRENDS

UPWARD TREND
- Data increases over time.
- Example: Growth of online shopping in India.

DOWNWARD TREND
- Data decreases over time.
- Example: CD/DVD demand falling due to streaming.

SEASONAL TREND
- Patterns repeat during particular periods.
- Example: Air-conditioner sales increasing every summer.

CYCLICAL TREND
- Changes occur over longer economic cycles.
- Examples:
  - Real estate cycles
  - Stock market cycles


HOW TO IDENTIFY PATTERNS & TRENDS

DATA VISUALIZATION
- Charts and plots reveal patterns visually.
- Example: A line chart showing an upward sales trend.

DESCRIPTIVE STATISTICS
- Mean, median, mode and standard deviation summarize data.
- Example: Average marks showing class performance.

CORRELATION ANALYSIS
- Measures the relationship between two variables.
- Example: Study hours vs marks.
- Can show positive correlation.

TIME SERIES ANALYSIS
- Studies data collected over time.
- Example: Banks analyzing yearly transaction trends.


APPLICATIONS OF PATTERN & TREND ANALYSIS

Business:
- Popular products
- Customer preferences
- Sales trends

Education:
- Attendance patterns
- Examination results

Healthcare:
- Disease trends
- Patient admission patterns

Banking:
- Spending behaviour
- Fraudulent transaction patterns

E-Commerce:
- Shopping habits
- Purchase sequences

Sports Analytics:
- Player performance
- Winning patterns


CASE STUDY — COLLEGE STUDENT PERFORMANCE

A college collected data from 1,000 students:
- Attendance
- Assignment scores
- Internal examination marks
- Final examination marks

Results:
- Pattern: Regular assignment completion -> higher marks.
- Pattern: Attendance above 75% -> significantly better results.
- Trend: Overall pass percentage improved every year.

Impact:
- Attendance monitoring introduced.
- Assignment tracking and academic counseling added.
- Student performance improved further.


CHALLENGES IN IDENTIFYING PATTERNS & TRENDS

LARGE AMOUNTS OF DATA
- Millions of records can be difficult to analyze thoroughly.

POOR DATA QUALITY
- Missing or incorrect data can produce false patterns.

CHANGING CUSTOMER BEHAVIOUR
- Patterns shift over time.
- Example: DVDs declining due to streaming.

SEASONAL VARIATIONS
- Seasonal effects can hide the actual underlying trend.


BENEFITS OF EXPLOITING PATTERNS & TRENDS

- Better and faster decision-making
- Improved forecasting
- Reduced risk
- Increased profits
- Fraud detection
- Competitive advantage


MODERN TECHNOLOGIES USED

- Excel
- Power BI
- Tableau
- Python
- R
- Machine Learning
- Artificial Intelligence
- Big Data technologies


TOPIC 3 KEY TAKEAWAYS

- Patterns are repeated relationships.
- Trends show the general direction of data over time.
- Patterns can be association, classification, sequential or clustering.
- Trends can be upward, downward, seasonal or cyclical.
- Visualization, statistics, correlation and time-series analysis help identify them.

TOPIC 4 — INTRODUCTION TO STATISTICAL CONCEPTS IN DATA ANALYSIS
==================================================

WHAT IS STATISTICS?

Statistics is the process of collecting, organizing, analyzing, interpreting and presenting data to make meaningful conclusions.


EXAMPLES OF STATISTICS

- A researcher calculates the average marks of students.
- A company predicts next month's sales.
- A hospital studies average patient recovery time.
- A bank estimates the risk of giving a loan.


WHY STATISTICS MATTERS IN DATA ANALYSIS

- Helps understand large datasets.
- Reduces complex data into simple summaries.
- Supports accurate decision-making.
- Helps detect patterns and trends.
- Measures uncertainty.
- Supports predictive models.


POPULATION VS SAMPLE

POPULATION
- The complete collection of individuals or observations.

Example:
A college has 5,000 students.
All 5,000 students are the population.

Studying the entire population can be expensive and time-consuming.

SAMPLE
- A smaller portion selected from the population for analysis.

Example:
Selecting 500 of the 5,000 students to study.

Election surveys often ask a sample of voters instead of everyone.


TYPES OF VARIABLES

QUALITATIVE / CATEGORICAL VARIABLES
- Describe qualities or categories.
- Examples:
  - Gender
  - Blood Group
  - Department
  - City

QUANTITATIVE / NUMERICAL VARIABLES
- Contain numbers that can be measured.
- Examples:
  - Salary
  - Marks
  - Weight
  - Height


LEVELS OF DATA MEASUREMENT

NOMINAL SCALE
- Names or labels categories.
- No meaningful calculations.
- Examples:
  - Gender
  - Religion
  - Blood Group

ORDINAL SCALE
- Categories have a meaningful order.
- Difference between categories is not necessarily measurable.
- Examples:
  - Poor
  - Average
  - Good
  - Excellent

INTERVAL SCALE
- Equal intervals.
- No true zero.
- Example:
  Temperature in Celsius.
- 0°C does not mean "no temperature."

RATIO SCALE
- Equal intervals with a true zero.
- Examples:
  - Height
  - Weight
  - Age
  - Salary
- All arithmetic operations are meaningful.


DESCRIPTIVE VS INFERENTIAL STATISTICS

DESCRIPTIVE STATISTICS
- Summarizes data.
- Uses tables, charts and averages.
- Describes existing data.
- Focuses on the current dataset.

Example:
Average marks of one class.

INFERENTIAL STATISTICS
- Makes predictions and conclusions.
- Uses probability and sampling.
- Generalizes from a sample to a population.
- Estimates characteristics of the population.

Example:
Predicting all students' performance from a sample.


MEASURES OF CENTRAL TENDENCY

MEAN
- Sum of values / Number of values.
- Example:
  Marks = 70, 80, 50
  Mean = 200 / 3

MEDIAN
- Middle value after arranging data in order.
- Useful when extreme values distort the mean.

MODE
- Most frequently occurring value.
- Example:
  10, 20, 20, 30
  Mode = 20

Applications:
- Schools use mean to calculate average marks.
- Median can be useful for house prices because very expensive homes can distort the average.
- Stores can use mode to find the most popular size or product.


MEASURES OF DISPERSION

RANGE
- Highest value - Lowest value.

Example:
40, 50, 60
Range = 60 - 40 = 20

VARIANCE
- Measures how far values are from the average.
- Small variance = values are close together.
- Large variance = values are more spread out.

STANDARD DEVIATION
- Measures the spread of values around the mean.
- Lower standard deviation means more consistency.
- Higher standard deviation means greater variation.


PROBABILITY

Probability is the chance that an event will happen.

It is expressed as a value between:
- 0 and 1
or
- 0% and 100%


CORRELATION BETWEEN VARIABLES

POSITIVE CORRELATION
- Both variables tend to move together.
- Example:
  Students who study more generally score higher.

NEGATIVE CORRELATION
- One variable increases while the other decreases.
- Example:
  Study time and free time may move in opposite directions.

NO CORRELATION
- No clear relationship exists between the two variables.
- Example:
  Roll number and examination marks.


WHAT IS A HYPOTHESIS?

A hypothesis is an assumption that can be tested using data.

Example:
"Students who attend more classes score better marks."

Testing process:
1. Collect attendance and marks data.
2. Use statistical methods to test whether the assumption holds true.


CASE STUDY — COLLEGE PERFORMANCE

A college wants to improve academic results using:
- Attendance
- Assignment marks
- Internal examination marks
- Final examination marks

Data analysis:
- Mean, median and standard deviation calculated.
- Pass percentage calculated.
- Relationship between attendance and marks studied.

Results:
- Average marks = 72
- Pass percentage = 88%
- Attendance above 85% was linked to higher scores.
- Attendance monitoring and counseling were introduced.
- Overall performance improved.


ADVANTAGES OF STATISTICS

- Simplifies complex data into clear summaries.
- Improves decision-making.
- Supports forecasting.
- Helps identify trends and patterns.
- Measures uncertainty.
- Reduces risk in business decisions.


LIMITATIONS OF STATISTICS

- Depends heavily on data quality.
- Incorrect data gives incorrect results.
- Cannot explain every real-world situation.
- Results may mislead if interpreted incorrectly.


TOPIC 4 KEY TAKEAWAYS

- Statistics helps collect, organize, analyze and interpret data.
- Population is the whole group; sample is a smaller representative part.
- Variables can be qualitative or quantitative.
- Measurement scales are nominal, ordinal, interval and ratio.
- Descriptive statistics summarizes data.
- Inferential statistics makes predictions or conclusions about a population.
- Mean, median, mode, range, variance and standard deviation describe data.
- Probability measures uncertainty.
- Correlation measures relationships between variables.
- A hypothesis is an assumption that can be tested using data.
- Statistics supports education, healthcare, banking, business and sports.
